# Code of conduct

Be nice. Assume the best about the person you are replying to.

Almost everyone who shows up here is trying to make cmux better, and most
friction is a missing sentence rather than bad intent. Ask what someone meant
before deciding they meant the worst thing.

## What we expect

- Talk about the code, not the person. "This drops the last keystroke" is a
  review. "Did you even test this" is not.
- Take the other reading seriously. If two people disagree about what cmux
  should do, say what each choice costs and let a maintainer decide.
- Say what you know and how you know it. Guesses are welcome when they are
  labeled as guesses.
- Let people leave. Nobody here owes anyone a reply, a fix, or a merge, and a
  quiet issue is not an insult. Our own reply queue runs long; we are working
  on it.
- English is a second language for many contributors. Read for meaning.

## What we do not accept

Harassment, slurs, or demeaning comments about who someone is: race,
ethnicity, nationality, sex, gender identity or expression, sexual
orientation, disability, age, religion, or appearance. Sexual attention or
imagery. Threats, doxxing, or following someone across threads to keep at
them. Deliberately wasting maintainer time: spam pull requests, engagement
farming, or reopening a settled decision to relitigate it.

Automation does not change the standard. If an agent opens a pull request
under your account, its output is yours, including its tone.

## Scope

This applies in this repository (issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions,
commit messages) and in cmux spaces elsewhere, including Discord, when you are
taking part as a cmux contributor.

## Reporting

Email [founders@manaflow.com](mailto:founders@manaflow.com) with links. If the
report is about a maintainer, say so in the subject line and it will be handled
by someone else. Reports stay between you and whoever handles it; we will not
share your name with the person you reported without asking you first.

We try to acknowledge every report. What happens next depends on what happened: usually
a private word, sometimes an edit or a lock, and for harassment or repeat
behavior a block from the repository. Losing access to a repository is not a
punishment we hand out for being wrong about a technical argument.

If someone is in immediate danger, contact your local emergency services
first. We cannot help with that.
