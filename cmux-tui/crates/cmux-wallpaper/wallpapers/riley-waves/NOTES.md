Current (after Bridget Riley's 1964 painting): point of view, geometry can vibrate without moving much. Dense near-horizontal stripes whose phase is bent by two slow travelling waves (one along x, wavelength `wavelength`; one oblique at 1.618x that, travelling the other way at 0.62x the rate) under an envelope down the field, so the stripe spacing tightens and opens and the eye supplies the vibration. The y-slopes are bounded (about 0.36 at the defaults) so stripes never fold.
Anti-aliasing: the phase field's gradient is analytic, so each pixel takes the exact 1 px box-filtered coverage of the square wave (the difference of the stripe's integral across the pixel's footprint), and fades to the flat mean (the duty) as the footprint grows from 0.35 to 0.75 stripes: no moire mush, no crawling, whatever the compression. No dpdx/dpdy.
Contrast: low on purpose (default 0.09 of the way from paper to ink in OKLab, about 0.06 L), because it sits under text and because fine drifting high-contrast stripes are a photosensitivity trigger (and Riley's own viewers reported seasickness). Cataract adds two-tone chroma: the light stripe leans to the accent's hue, the dark one to the opposite, by up to 0.05 chroma at nearly the same L, which reads as warm/cool shimmer without an Albers vibrating edge.
Motion: wave crests travel about 3 points a second and stripes move vertically at most ~2 points a second at speed 1; the envelope drifts slower still; the two waves move in opposite directions, so there is no coherent full-field flow. The pointer is a soft lens (radius 120 points): y' = y - bow (y - py) exp(-r^2/R^2), so stripes bow apart around it, eased with presence; `bow` is capped at 0.7 so the lens never folds the stripes.
Light and dark: OKLab mixing from the theme's paper toward its ink, the same contrast either way. Final color has a static triangular +-1 LSB dither.
Still: time 30 s, pointer at (0.6, 0.46).
Cost per pixel: 6 sin/cos and 1 exp, ~60 arithmetic for the phase field, lens and filter; color (3 OKLab conversions, back to RGB, dither) ~150 ALU: ~260 ALU per pixel, no loops, no textures.
Budget: scale 0.75 (a 1440 x 900 point window on a 2x screen is 2.9 M pixels) at 20 fps: ~0.76 G ops a frame, estimated ~0.3 ms on an M1-class GPU; not measured on a Mac yet. Scale 0.75 rather than 0.5 because stripe edges are the whole picture: at 0.5 the bilinear upscale softens every edge by a point, which reads as blur on a 4 to 7 point period; the program is cheap enough to afford it.
Params (8): period (points), duty (dark share), contrast, amplitude (points), wavelength (points), speed, chroma (two-tone), bow (pointer lens).
