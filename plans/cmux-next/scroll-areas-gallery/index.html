Scroll areas: three interaction designs

Design note: plans/cmux-next/scroll-areas-DESIGN.md. Every demo is clickable: click a pane to focus it, use the buttons (or the shown keys while this page has focus), and scroll sideways with the trackpad or Shift+wheel. The general idea: a split and a strip are one container with two sizing rules ("fit" or "scroll"). A scroll area is a "scroll" container below the screen.

Nothing new to learn. Split as today (Cmd-D). When a split cannot give every pane its minimum width, it scrolls instead of squashing. This works in the docked column on the right, which today can only squash. Turn the toggle off to see today's behavior. Cmd-Opt-Left/Right still moves to the neighbor pane, and focus scrolls the pane into view.

An explicit box. "Make Scroll Area" wraps the focused pane in a scroll area (blue outline). Inside it, "New Column in Area" adds a column that scrolls inside the area only, the way Ctrl-Cmd-X adds a screen column. Powerful, but "New Column" now has two meanings. Same-axis areas never nest directly (rule S1), and at most three scroll levels deep (S2).

Two workspaces, one view. Cmd-click a second workspace in the sidebar, then "View Side by Side". Each workspace keeps its own tabs and focus; the view only shows them together in one strip. Closing the view never closes a workspace. This is a view, never ownership: a workspace never contains another. Highest risk of confusion; recommended to wait.

A. Overflow splits (Recommended)

  • No new words, no new commands: a scrollbar shows only when a split overflows.
  • Fixes the real pain: docked and narrow columns no longer squash splits.
  • Client only for step 1: no daemon or wire change; off by default behind layout.scrollAreas.
  • Same reveal, snap and gesture rules as the column strip (one reducer).

B. Explicit scroll areas

  • Most flexible; needs a new store node, ops, TLA+ and actions on every surface.
  • "New Column" means "in the strip that holds focus": a second meaning to learn.
  • Builds on A's geometry later; no rewrite.

C. Side-by-side workspaces

  • Answers "a workspace with two workspaces" without nesting ownership.
  • New rules for sidebar, close, unread and restore; most confusing.
  • Recommended: decide after A is dogfooded.

Shared rules

  • Cmd-Opt-arrows: geometric neighbor (Ghostty semantics), unchanged.
  • Horizontal gestures: innermost area that can move, owner fixed per gesture.
  • Vertical wheel stays with the terminal (scrollback).
  • Offsets are view state; nothing new persists in step 1.
